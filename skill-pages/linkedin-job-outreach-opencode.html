LinkedIn Job Outreach

Create one-to-one LinkedIn drafts that are truthful, specific, useful to the recipient, and ready for the user to review. Never send, schedule, connect, follow, or publish.

Read [references/outreach-playbook.md](references/outreach-playbook.md) when choosing an audience, building a sequence, or drafting multiple message types.

Intake

Collect only what is needed:

  • direct employer posting URL or complete posting, company, role, location, and requisition ID;
  • whether the user applied, application date, channel, and current status;
  • recipient name, current role, public profile URL, and audience type;
  • real relationship context: stranger, follower, shared group, mutual contact, former colleague, or prior conversation;
  • one primary goal: confirm the role/process, learn about the team or culture, understand role priorities, find the correct recruiter, or explore a referral;
  • two or three verified candidate facts that make the outreach relevant;
  • privacy boundaries and preferred voice.

Ask concise follow-up questions for missing facts. Never infer a relationship, mutual connection, application status, accomplishment, or recipient responsibility.

Verify before messaging

  1. Confirm the opening on the employer's official careers site when available. Preserve the direct URL, requisition ID, and retrieval date.
  2. Compare the LinkedIn poster, company page, recruiter profile, and any verification signals with the official employer identity.
  3. Flag mismatched domains, payment requests, early requests for sensitive information, urgent off-platform moves, vague duties, or implausible compensation.
  4. Treat a contact's reply as context, not definitive proof. If the official posting cannot be found, label the role unverified and draft a neutral verification question rather than implying fraud.
  5. Stop and advise the user not to share more information if scam indicators appear.

Choose the audience

  • Recruiter or talent partner: confirm whether the requisition is active, identify the responsible recruiting team, ask about process or timing, and make the submitted application easy to locate.
  • Hiring manager: connect verified experience to one role priority, ask one informed question about outcomes or team needs, and mention the submitted application without demanding review.
  • Current employee or potential teammate: ask about lived team experience, collaboration, leadership behavior, or how stated values show up in work. Do not ask for confidential information.
  • Leader or executive: ask one concise strategic or organizational question only when their scope is relevant. Do not use executive outreach as an escalation tactic.
  • Warm contact: acknowledge the real relationship and ask whether they are comfortable sharing context or explaining the official referral process.

Do not contact everyone at once. Recommend the single best first recipient and explain why.

Sequence the request

  1. Start with relevance: name the exact role and why this person is a sensible contact.
  2. Disclose that the user has already applied when true.
  3. Ask one focused, answerable question.
  4. Make the message easy to ignore or decline without guilt.
  5. Ask for a referral only when the relationship is warm or the person has engaged and has enough context to make an informed choice.
  6. Ask about the official employee-referral process; never ask someone to falsely endorse qualifications they cannot verify.
  7. Send at most one polite follow-up after a reasonable interval, then stop unless the recipient replies.

Draft the message kit

Return:

  1. Recommended recipient and purpose with a one-sentence rationale.
  2. Public verification status: Verified, Unverified, or Concerning, with evidence and unresolved questions.
  3. Recommended message in the user's voice, suitable for the current LinkedIn composer.
  4. Connection-note version only if a connection request is appropriate.
  5. One follow-up that adds context rather than repeating the request.
  6. Referral ask only when the sequencing rule permits it; otherwise explain what should happen first.
  7. Questions to avoid, including confidential company information, private compensation, protected personal information, or demands for influence.
  8. User review checklist for names, role, requisition, claims, tone, and privacy.

Prefer one recommended draft over a pile of interchangeable variants. Offer a warmer or more direct rewrite only when the user asks.

Message quality rules

  • Use plain language and a human voice. Avoid flattery, corporate jargon, generic praise, desperation, guilt, and AI-sounding filler.
  • Reference one genuine detail from the posting, recipient's public work, or real relationship context.
  • Keep the ask small. Do not combine legitimacy, culture, referral, résumé review, and a call request in one first message.
  • Do not claim the recipient is the hiring manager or recruiter without evidence.
  • Do not scrape private profile data, bypass LinkedIn controls, generate bulk lists, automate invitations, or mass-send templated messages.
  • Do not promise a reply, referral, interview, or hiring advantage.
  • Human approval is mandatory before every connection request or message.
