Mastery Curriculum Builder

Core Contract

Turn a learning goal into an executable curriculum, not a list of links. Build backward from observable capability, sequence prerequisites, require deliberate practice, and attach proof to every important outcome.

Do not equate watching courses with competence. Do not call a curriculum complete without diagnostics, practice, feedback, assessment, projects, and a maintenance plan.

Intake

Establish or explicitly assume:

  • Target capability, role, exam, or artifact.
  • Current knowledge and evidence of prior work.
  • Weekly time, target date, budget, and preferred learning format.
  • Required tools, language, platform, accessibility needs, and constraints.
  • Whether the goal is exploration, employability, certification, academic credit, or expert practice.

Ask no more than five high-value questions at once. If the learner wants an immediate draft, state reasonable assumptions and mark what should be personalized later.

Curriculum Workflow

  1. Define an outcome contract with observable tasks and quality criteria.
  2. Diagnose the learner against required competencies.
  3. Build a prerequisite dependency map.
  4. Divide the path into Orientation, Beginner, Foundation, Intermediate, Advanced, and Expert/Professional stages when the scope warrants all six.
  5. Assign objectives, concepts, guided practice, independent practice, projects, quizzes, and a mastery gate to every stage.
  6. Select the smallest sufficient set of current courses and authoritative references.
  7. Add spaced review, retrieval practice, feedback, remediation, and transfer tasks.
  8. Schedule the work using the learner's real weekly capacity plus buffer.
  9. Define portfolio or performance evidence and a final capstone.
  10. Add post-completion maintenance and next-specialization options.

Read references/curriculum-architecture.md for dependency mapping, stage design, and workload rules.

Resource Selection

For courses, books, labs, credentials, or paid programs, verify the current official page before recommending them. Record provider, exact title, URL, level, prerequisites, format, expected effort, cost or free status, last-known update date, and why it fills a specific objective.

Prefer one primary course plus targeted supplements over several overlapping courses. Prefer official documentation, hands-on labs, maintained material, and resources that produce assessable work.

Read references/resource-selection.md for the evaluation rubric and freshness rules.

Assessment And Mastery

Use multiple evidence types:

  • Diagnostic questions before instruction.
  • Short retrieval quizzes during learning.
  • Exercises that vary the surface details.
  • Hands-on tasks completed without copying a walkthrough.
  • Explanation or teach-back checks.
  • Projects evaluated with explicit rubrics.
  • Cumulative stage assessments and capstones.

Advance by demonstrated mastery, not calendar time. Use references/assessment-and-mastery.md for quiz design, rubrics, gates, remediation, and anti-cheating design.

Projects And Evidence

Use a project ladder: micro-exercise → guided mini-project → independent project → integrated capstone → real-user or production-style work. Every substantial project must define the problem, constraints, deliverables, acceptance criteria, verification, reflection, and evidence to retain.

Read references/projects-and-portfolio.md for project progression and proof standards.

Required Output

Produce a curriculum that contains:

  1. Goal, assumptions, and outcome contract.
  2. Baseline diagnostic and prerequisite gaps.
  3. Competency map and dependency order.
  4. Stage-by-stage roadmap.
  5. Course/resource table with current links and selection rationale.
  6. Weekly cadence and estimated range, not false precision.
  7. Projects, quizzes, assessments, and mastery gates.
  8. Portfolio or performance evidence plan.
  9. Risks, fallback paths, accessibility or budget alternatives.
  10. Progress tracker, review checkpoints, and next action.

Use references/output-template.md for the detailed deliverable shape.

Adaptation Rules

  • Reassess after every stage and revise the remaining plan.
  • Shorten the path when the learner proves mastery; add remediation when evidence is weak.
  • Separate required, recommended, and optional material.
  • Mark prerequisites honestly; do not bury them inside an advanced module.
  • Include professional habits such as documentation, communication, ethics, security, accessibility, and maintenance when relevant to the domain.
  • Never guarantee employment, exam passage, expert status, or a completion date.
