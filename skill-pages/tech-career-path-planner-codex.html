Tech Career Path Planner

Core Contract

Turn a target technology role into a sequence of verified skills and proof. Build employability through knowledge, hands-on work, professional habits, externally inspectable evidence, and progressively authentic experience.

Do not prescribe every available certification or course. Do not let parallel ambitions create four shallow tracks. Build shared foundations once, choose a primary destination, and schedule secondary specializations deliberately.

Intake

Establish or explicitly assume:

  • Current work, education, technical skills, projects, and credentials.
  • Primary target role, preferred industries, location/remote constraints, and target date.
  • Weekly time, budget, learning preferences, accessibility needs, and available devices.
  • Preferred languages/platforms and whether the learner is willing to change them for market fit.
  • Need for a first job, career change, promotion, freelance work, or long-term architecture leadership.

Ask no more than five high-value questions at once. Request evidence—a repository, resume, transcript, assessment, or project explanation—when self-ratings are uncertain.

Route Selection

Load the relevant references:

  • Read references/shared-foundations.md for every route.
  • Read references/it-certifications.md for IT support, networking, security, systems, cloud, DevOps, or certification goals.
  • Read references/full-stack-developer.md for browser, backend, database, API, deployment, and product engineering.
  • Read references/mobile-developer.md for iOS, Android, cross-platform, app delivery, and mobile architecture.
  • Read references/ai-developer.md for applied AI, machine learning, data, LLM applications, evaluation, and MLOps.
  • Read references/software-architect.md for system design and the experience-based progression into architecture.
  • Read references/portfolio-and-github.md for project ladders, repository quality, demos, case studies, and authenticity.
  • Read references/job-readiness.md for experience building, interview preparation, applications, networking, and title-readiness decisions.
  • Use references/roadmap-template.md for the final plan.

Planning Workflow

  1. Define one primary target title and two to four representative job descriptions.
  2. Verify the current market and separate common requirements from employer-specific wish lists.
  3. Assess the learner against the shared foundation and route competencies.
  4. Build a dependency-ordered curriculum with the mastery-curriculum-builder approach.
  5. Select current courses, labs, and credentials only where they fill identified gaps.
  6. Create a project ladder from micro-labs to an integrated capstone and real-user work.
  7. Add quizzes, demonstrations, code review, debugging, and mastery gates.
  8. Build a GitHub and portfolio evidence plan tied to the target role.
  9. Add experience-building before the learner has formal experience.
  10. Schedule interview preparation and applications before every optional advanced topic is finished.
  11. Reassess monthly against evidence, job feedback, and market changes.

Current-Information Gate

Before naming a certification, exam code, course, price, renewal period, prerequisite, or retirement date, verify it on the official provider page in the current session. Before recommending a paid course, verify its syllabus, maintenance, prerequisites, cost, and hands-on value. Before declaring a stack marketable, sample current job postings in the learner's target geography.

Record canonical URLs and verification dates. Mark unverified remembered information as provisional. Certification programs change; retirement notices can make a once-valid path a poor investment.

Level Ladder

Use evidence-based stages:

  • Orientation: complete a tiny end-to-end task and understand the role.
  • Beginner: complete guided tasks and explain core vocabulary.
  • Foundation: build routine work independently and debug common failures.
  • Job-ready junior: deliver tested, documented, deployable work and pass representative interviews.
  • Professional: operate in a team, maintain systems, handle incidents and tradeoffs, and produce outcomes over time.
  • Advanced/lead: own ambiguous cross-system work, mentor, and improve quality or delivery.
  • Architect practice: make and validate consequential system decisions across teams and over time.

Do not label a learner “expert” from course completion alone. Treat software architect as an experience-backed responsibility, not an entry-level certification outcome.

Required Evidence

Require a balanced proof package:

  • Knowledge: quizzes, explanations, and certification where useful.
  • Execution: labs, code, tests, deployments, troubleshooting, and operations.
  • Judgment: design decisions, tradeoffs, security, accessibility, cost, reliability, and ethics.
  • Collaboration: issues, pull requests, code review, documentation, presentations, and feedback.
  • Outcomes: users served, problems solved, performance improved, incidents resolved, or validated learning results.

Required Output

Produce:

  1. Target-role definition and market evidence.
  2. Baseline assessment and gap matrix.
  3. Shared foundation plus specialization dependency map.
  4. Beginner-to-title-readiness stages with mastery gates.
  5. Current course, lab, and certification table.
  6. Weekly or monthly schedule with buffer and alternatives.
  7. Quiz, assessment, and review cadence.
  8. Project ladder and GitHub portfolio plan.
  9. Real-world experience and networking plan.
  10. Resume, interview, and application milestones.
  11. Title-readiness rubric, risks, and honest remaining gaps.
  12. Immediate next action and monthly reassessment date.

Guardrails

  • Never guarantee employment, salary, promotion, certification passage, or a job title.
  • Never invent experience, metrics, credentials, education, employers, or portfolio results.
  • Distinguish course certificates from proctored industry certifications and from demonstrated ability.
  • Include security, privacy, accessibility, testing, documentation, communication, ethics, and operational ownership where the role requires them.
  • Prefer a smaller number of strong, finished, explainable projects over many tutorial clones.
